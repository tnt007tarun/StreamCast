Bronte Creek · Oakville · Halton

Bronte runs last, and then runs again

Two things make Bronte worth the drive. It is the last salmon river of the Ontario season — fish push in through late September and the run holds into November, weeks after the eastern creeks have gone quiet. And it is a genuinely good spring steelhead creek, December through April. If your season is ending everywhere else, drive west.

Written from the water · Gauge: Bronte Creek at Zimmerman · Updated September 2026

Salmon fishing Bronte Creek — the last run of the Ontario season.

Two seasons, at opposite ends of the year

Most rivers here give you one window. Bronte gives you two, and they are about as far apart as they could be — salmon from late September into November, then steelhead from December through April. Between them, that is more than half the year on one creek.

Why the salmon run late

Lake Ontario does not cool evenly. The eastern basin is deeper and colder and turns over first, which is why the Ganaraska and Bowmanville see fish from mid-August. The western end, where Bronte sits, is shallower and warmer and holds its summer temperature weeks longer. Salmon wait offshore until the lake lets them in.

The practical version: come two to three weeks later than you would to the eastern creeks, and three to four weeks later than the calendar suggests. Anglers who fish Bronte on a Ganaraska schedule arrive to an empty creek and conclude the run is poor.

The creek right now

Bronte fishes best between 2 and 10 m³/s. It is a modest creek, so low and clear is the usual autumn problem rather than high water — and low, clear water with salmon in it means spooky fish and long leaders. Low flow also decides whether a couple of the crossings are sensible, so it is worth a look before you plan a route.

Before you go

Below Lakeshore Road West, including Bronte Harbour: open all year. This is the only reach with no closing date.

Lakeshore Road West up to Highway 407, including Bronte Creek Provincial Park: extended season, fourth Saturday in April to December 31.

Above Highway 407, in the Niagara Escarpment headwaters: the zone-wide season applies — fourth Saturday in April to September 30. Closed from October 1, which is exactly when Bronte’s run gets going. The headwaters are a spring and summer brook trout fishery, not a salmon one.

Confirm the current rules in the Ontario fishing regulations for Zone 16 before you fish.

What it's actually like

Bronte runs through what is close to a mini-ravine, and it is a genuinely lovely place to spend a morning. The lower and middle creek is not heavily wooded — you are down in the valley with the banks rising either side rather than under a canopy — and then it changes character once you reach Bronte Creek Provincial Park and the conservation land, which is beautiful in a different way.

It is wadeable for most of its length, which is not true of every river on this list. A couple of spots should only be crossed when flow is genuinely low — worth checking the gauge before you plan a crossing rather than working it out mid-river.

It is also a better steelhead creek than its reputation suggests, through the spring, and I have seen lake-run brown trout in here as well. If you only think of Bronte as a late salmon river you are missing most of its year.

Where to fish it

Bronte Harbour and the pier. Where fish stage from late August, and open all year. Spoons and spinners at dawn and dusk before they commit to the creek.

The lower creek, below Lakeshore Road West. Year-round water, and your only option once the calendar turns.

Bronte Creek Provincial Park. The main in-river reach through October and November. The park is 684 hectares — almost exactly the size of Central Park — so there is a great deal of creek to spread out along.

Lions Valley Park and Lowville Park. Further up, quieter, and worth walking.

Spring steelhead

The steelhead season runs roughly December through April and is, to my mind, underrated here. Below Lakeshore Road West the water is open all year, which covers the whole of it. Lake-run brown trout turn up too, often when you are not expecting them.

Spring is also when the wading advice matters most. High dirty water after snowmelt is when the fish move and when the crossings get genuinely unwise — the short above was filmed in exactly those conditions.

Twelve Mile Creek

Bronte was not always called Bronte. On the early maps it was Twelve Mile Creek, being twelve miles from what was then reckoned the head of Lake Ontario — the same counting that gave Oakville its Sixteen Mile Creek. The Mississaugas knew it as ishkwessin, “that which lies at the end”.

The village at the mouth was founded in 1834 and named for Horatio Nelson, who held the title Duke of Bronte. The word itself is Greek — bronté, thunder.

It became a working harbour fast. By the 1850s Bronte was exporting wheat, building schooners — the 172-ton Peerless was launched here in 1853 — and running a commercial fishery and a stonehooking trade, with the population up around 550. Then the Grand Trunk Railway arrived in 1856 and bypassed the village entirely. The harbour trade collapsed and the population fell to roughly 220. Bronte stayed a fishing village, on a much smaller scale, into the 1950s.

The harbour has one more piece of history worth knowing: it was an entry point on the Underground Railroad, and some of those who arrived settled at Bronte and built homes on both banks of the creek.

Common questions

When do salmon run in Bronte Creek?

Later than anywhere else on this side of the lake. Fish stage off the harbour from late August, push in through late September, and the run holds into November.

Can you fish Bronte in November?

Below Lakeshore Road West, yes — that reach is open all year. Between Lakeshore and Highway 407, including the Provincial Park, the season runs to December 31. Above the 407, trout season closed on September 30.

Why does it run so late?

The western end of Lake Ontario is shallower and warmer, and cools last. Salmon stage offshore until the lake lets them in.
