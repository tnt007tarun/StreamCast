Bowmanville Creek · Clarington

When do salmon run in Bowmanville Creek?

Chinook stage off Port Darlington from early July and push into the creek from mid-to-late August, peaking mid-September through mid-October. It is a small creek with a big run — and the reason the fish get upstream at all is that a few hundred volunteers once carried them over a dam by hand.

Written from the water · Gauge: Bowmanville Creek at Bowmanville · Updated September 2026

Bowmanville is a creek, not a river, and almost everything about fishing it follows from that. It rises fast, drops fast, and holds a lot of fish in not much water. I check the gauge the day after rain here, not three days later — miss the window and you are back to low and clear.

The creek right now

Bowmanville fishes best between 1 and 8 m³/s. Below that the fish stack in the deeper pools and get very spooky; above it, fish the slower inside edges rather than the main push.

After solid rain it takes one to two days to drop — not the several days the Ganaraska needs. Aim for the back half of that window, when levels are falling but the water still carries colour.

Field note · 8 September 2026

I fished it last weekend and there were very few fish — far fewer than I would normally expect in early September. What makes that odd is that we had good rain beforehand, which usually brings them in.

My guess is that a body of fish ran on that water and kept going, and by the time the creek dropped clear enough to fish they were already well upstream. On a creek this size the window between “too high” and “they have moved through” can be a single day. It is the trade-off for water that clears in one to two days rather than several.

I still got into one, so they were not all gone. But if you fished it early this month and wondered where everything was, that is my read.

Bowmanville sits in the eastern group, so it runs early — alongside the Ganaraska and Wilmot, and two to three weeks ahead of the Credit or Bronte. If you fish the Toronto rivers and come out here on your usual schedule, you will be late.

Before you go

No fishing within 120 metres upstream or downstream of the fish ladder at the Goodyear Dam. This is enforced, and it exists for a good reason — see below. The ladder is roughly one kilometre north of the Baseline Road parking lot on the Bowmanville Valley Trail.

Standard Zone 17 seasons and limits apply. Confirm the current rules in the Ontario fishing regulations before you fish.

Why there is a fish ladder at all

The Goodyear Dam is about three metres of concrete, and for years it was effectively the end of the run. An older passage had been built for trout, but when Chinook were introduced they simply could not make the jump. They piled up at the base, exhausted themselves, and died there. The estimate at the time was that only about one per cent of the big salmon made it upstream to spawn.

So people started lifting them over by hand. A local named Dave Lawson had rigged a bag-and-pulley system years earlier for trout; in 2012, with a new bypass channel behind schedule and an early run arriving, volunteers from the community group Valleys 2000 stood in the pool below the dam and moved 5,540 fish over it in nets and sacks. The following year, while construction continued, they did it again — more than 10,000 fish.

The bypass channel finally opened on 16 December 2013: four tiered pools, about $450,000, most of it raised locally. That is what the 120-metre no-fishing zone protects. Given what it took to get built, it is worth giving it room.

What a morning here is actually like

It gets busy in October, but it is a different kind of busy from Port Hope. Quieter, more spread out, and shared with people who are not fishing at all — the valley trail is paved and level, and you will pass families, dog walkers and photographers on the way to the water.

It is also, simply, a lovely place to spend a morning. The last time I was down there a friend logged the calls of around fifteen different bird species in two hours, without trying especially hard. That is not a normal thing to be able to say about a salmon river twenty minutes from the 401.

You can stand at the ladder and watch fish work their way up it. Plenty of people come down just for that, and never make a cast.

Where to fish it

Port Darlington and the harbour hold staging fish from July, before they commit to the creek. Spoons and spinners at dawn and dusk.

The lower creek, below the dam, is the main event through September and October. Float-fished roe or beads through the deeper pools. Mind the 120-metre boundary around the ladder.

Above the bypass, fish that have made it up are more spread out and the water is quieter. Worth walking for.

There is also a wheelchair-accessible fishing spot on the trail south of the Goodyear Dam and north of Spry Avenue, put in where studies showed fish actually congregate — which is a more thoughtful piece of design than most rivers manage.

Barber's Creek

It was not always called Bowmanville. The creek was originally Barber's Creek, after the early settler Augustus Barber. Around 1820 the Burk family — one of three Loyalist families who arrived in the 1790s — built a saw and grist mill on its banks, and the village that grew around it was called Darlington Mills. By 1830 the village had been renamed Bowmanville, and the creek followed.

Through the nineteenth century that water powered a remarkable amount of industry for a creek this size: potteries, a distillery, a soap works, an oatmeal mill, a foundry, a tannery, woodworking and machine shops. The Vanstone Mill still stands at the western approach to downtown, and by the 1880s it was turning out 150 barrels of flour a day.

The creek itself starts on the Oak Ridges Moraine, which is the useful part for anglers: that groundwater recharge is what keeps a creek this small cold enough to hold salmon and trout at all.

Common questions

Can you fish at the Bowmanville fish ladder?

No. Fishing is prohibited within 120 metres upstream and downstream of it. You can watch, and it is worth watching, but not fish that stretch.

How long does the creek take to clear after rain?

One to two days — much faster than the Ganaraska. Time your trip for the falling half of that window.

Is it crowded?

Busy in October, but noticeably quieter than Port Hope, and the valley draws plenty of people who are there for the walk rather than the fish.
